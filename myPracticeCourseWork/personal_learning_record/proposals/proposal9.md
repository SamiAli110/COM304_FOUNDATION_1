# Council Proposal 9

## Explanation of this proposal

This proposal recommends strengthening the council’s cybersecurity posture to protect systems, services, and personal data against cybercrime. Local authorities are frequent targets for cyber attacks such as phishing, ransomware, data breaches, Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS) attacks, and insider threats. A successful attack could disrupt essential public services, cause financial loss, damage public trust, and put sensitive information at risk.

Cybersecurity hardening involves multiple strategies, including:
1. Improving network security
2. Implementing multi-factor authentication
3. Enforcing strong password policies
4. Regularly updating and patching systems
A layered, “defence-in-depth” approach ensures that even if one security control is bypassed, additional protections are still in place.
This proposal aligns with UK public-sector cybersecurity guidance and aims to reduce risks while supporting safe digital transformation.

## Initial Analysis of the Proposal
Improving the council’s cybersecurity stance is essential for protecting critical services and public data. Cyber threats continue to grow in complexity, and local authorities are at high risk due to their reliance on digital systems. While cybersecurity improvements require investment in staff training, monitoring tools, and system upgrades, the long-term benefits far outweigh the costs. A proactive approach will help prevent service outages, data breaches, and reputational damage.
